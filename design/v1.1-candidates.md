# Candidates for v1.1

**Nothing here is implemented, and nothing here should be implemented on the strength of this
document.** These are design principles recorded so they survive the conversation that produced them
(Standard 18 R5). The trigger for acting on any of them is evidence from applying v1.0.0 to a real
mathematical research repository — not the fact that they are written down.

The v1.0.0 position is deliberate: 22 standards, a catalog where roughly a third of the rules have no
detector by design, and a stopping point chosen because the remaining gaps need judgement rather than
engineering. Adding to it speculatively would violate the third testing principle
([design/testing-principles.md](testing-principles.md)) and the guidance that every guard should come
from a real failure.

**The entries from §0 to §0l are evidence-backed.** They came from applying v1.0.0 to two real
repositories and from one genuine operator error against this repository; each has a preserved
artifact and a reproduced mechanism. Everything from §1 onwards remains speculative and is marked as
such. The distinction is the point of this file: an idea that sounds useful and an idea that has been
observed failing are not the same kind of thing, and only the second earns implementation.

### Producers, and what confirmation means here

Two independent adopters now exist. Neither record cites the other:
[`RiemannHypothesis`](../artifacts/adoption/) across seven phases, and `PvsNP` in that repository's
own `docs/standards-adoption/`. The comparison is
[`artifacts/adoption/two-adopters.md`](../artifacts/adoption/two-adopters.md).

An entry marked **confirmed by two producers** was observed failing in both, on different subject
matter, by agents who had not seen each other's findings. That is the bar for treating a defect as a
property of the framework rather than of one project's conventions.

| §0a unknown args mutate | **RH + PvsNP** | high |

**§0b and §0i are the most urgent**, and they are the same defect arriving by two routes: a verdict
that can only be cleared by an act the standards themselves discourage. §0b clears by editing honest
prose; §0i clears by writing *less* explanation in a field. Both are unclearable without a Standard 21
violation, and both are invariants.

**§0e ranks above §0f and §0g.** Those two make attestations painful to author. §0e undermines what an
accepted attestation *means over time*: without a usable path to the digest, `review → attestation →
bound to reviewed state → goes stale on change` collapses into `review → unbound assurance → continues
looking current`. A formatting burden is a cost; an assurance that silently stops being true is a
defect of the same kind the framework exists to prevent.

## 0. Data sufficiency: a detector with no subject must not report a pass

**Status: evidence-backed, confirmed by two producers.** It has a preserved real-repository artifact,
an observably incorrect machine-readable representation, and a concrete failure mechanism. Evidence:
[`artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-pre-adoption.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-pre-adoption.md) §2,
raw output in `artifacts/adoption/rh-baseline-validate.json`.

**The second producer supplies a clean instance and a third category.** In PvsNP,
`literature.resolvable-identifiers` passed having examined nothing: it checks DOI and arXiv *format*,
and that project's 16 references carry neither. It is reported identically to
`computation.float-as-exact`, which scanned both cited programs and found only integer floor division
against evidence claiming exact arithmetic. One row is a real check on real evidence; the other is a
check with no subject; the output cannot tell them apart.

That adopter also found a category this entry does not cover — passes that are about the *framework*
rather than the adopter. Recorded separately as
[§0h](#0h-the-framework-audits-itself-inside-the-adopters-compliance-score), because the fix is
different: §0 needs a data-sufficiency state, §0h needs a subject.

### The observation

Pointing v1.0.0 at an uninitialized real repository produces an envelope that simultaneously says
`status: NOT_EVALUATED` and `score: 95`, with 51 results marked `passed / evaluated`. Twenty-three of
those passes concern mathematical structures that do not exist — including, with some irony,
`claims.silent-promotion` and `evidence.skipped-never-passed`.

The status field, the exit code, and the human renderer are all correct. The JSON is not, and the
JSON is what a CI job or dashboard consumes.

### The mechanism

The framework's central invariant — *a rule nothing evaluated is skipped, never passed* — is
implemented at the level of **detector availability**: is there a check for this rule? It is not
implemented at the level of **data sufficiency**: did the check have anything to examine?

```text
detector exists + examined the relevant evidence + found no violation      = pass
detector exists + the prerequisite evidence does not exist                 ≠ pass
```

The second must never become evidence of compliance. No fixture reached it, because every fixture has
a ledger and the framework's own repository declares those rules not-applicable — so they surface as
`not-applicable`, not as `passed`. It took an adopter with real mathematics and no ledger.

### What a fix has to get right, and what it must not do

- Each detector needs to report **not-run-for-want-of-data** as a third outcome, distinct from both
  *found nothing* and *no detector exists*. Reusing `not-evaluated` is probably right; inventing a
  fourth disposition probably is not.
- The prerequisite must be per-detector, not global. `formal.axiom-disclosure` has a subject whenever
  proof-assistant sources exist, ledger or no; `claims.silent-promotion` does not.
- **Do not solve it by making the absent prerequisite a failure.** A project legitimately without a
  claims ledger yet should see *not evaluated*, not a red build for every claim rule.
- The score's denominator must exclude data-insufficient rules, exactly as it already excludes
  undetected ones.

### The third dimension this exposes

v1.0 deliberately separated the compliance score from framework coverage, so that a coverage
improvement could not look like a compliance improvement. The baseline shows a third quantity that is
neither: **evaluation coverage for a particular run** — of the rules that have detectors, how many had
a subject to examine. Catalog detector coverage and actually-evaluated evidence are different things,
and the gap between them is exactly what an adopting project needs to see.

Not designing that metric yet. Recording that adoption demonstrated the concept.

### Sharpened by initialization

Running `init --apply` on the same repository ruled out the obvious fix. A ledger file now exists —
the template scaffold, carrying one worked example claim — and the 23 vacuous passes are still
passes, now evaluated against a fictional theorem about `x² ≥ 0` while 982 real declarations remain
invisible. The prerequisite test therefore **cannot be "does the ledger file exist."** A file existed
and the answer got less true, because the subject the detectors found was one the tool had written
itself minutes earlier. Whatever replaces it has to distinguish a project's claims from the
framework's scaffolding — which is the same boundary the mode-detection code already draws for
directories, and for the same reason
([`init.mjs` `hasContent`](../scripts/init.mjs): "a tool must never treat its own scaffolding as
evidence about the project"). Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-post-init.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-post-init.md) §2.

The complete causal trace is in
[`rh-postinit-characterization.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-postinit-characterization.md) Part 2.
One null check — `if (!ledger) return;`, repeated in sixteen detector functions — gates **27 rules**.
Twenty-four of them passed before and after; what changed is that they went from *not executing* to
*executing over a claim the framework wrote*. That is the worse of the two states, because the
evidence trail is now genuine and only the subject is fictional. A reader of the JSON has no signal
separating those passes from passes over the project's real mathematics.

### The broader question this forces

Narrower framings — "remove `CLM-0001`", "ship the example commented out" — do not reach the defect.
`init` inserted a positive mathematical claim, at proved rank, with an evidence citation and a
promotion history, into an existing research repository, and the framework then evaluated that
repository partly on its own authored claim. The question v1.1 has to answer is:

> **What epistemic assertions, if any, may initialization create on behalf of an adopter?**

For a standards system whose subject is provenance and claim integrity, a bootstrap that manufactures
claims is a category error regardless of whether any individual detector then behaves correctly. The
principle is already written in this codebase for directories and was never extended to file
contents.

## 0a. Unknown or invalid CLI arguments must fail closed before any mutation

**Status: evidence-backed.** Source is an operator error rather than the specimen: an attempt to
check flag spelling with `init --help` ran an apply against this repository. Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-post-init.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-post-init.md) §4.

**Confirmed by a second producer, with the worse instance.** PvsNP reproduced it deliberately in a
scratch fixture: `init <dir> --dryrun` — a one-character typo for `--dry-run` — is ignored, **writes
all seven paths**, and exits 0. `--no-such-flag` behaves identically. This is the exact failure the
requirement was stated broadly to cover: an operator who believes they are previewing has instead
scaffolded.

### The observation

`--help` is recognised only as a *subcommand*. `init --help` parses as a bare `init` with an
unrecognised flag silently ignored, and applies. It created four artifacts in the framework
repository before anyone had decided to run anything.

### The defect, stated generally

> A mutating command accepts unrecognised arguments and can therefore perform a mutation when the
> operator intended a non-mutating or invalid invocation.

`init --help` is the observed case. `--dryrun`, `--dry_run` and `--dry-run=true` are the same shape:
`--dry-run` is tested by exact presence, so any near-miss spelling applies instead of previewing.
They demonstrate the risk and **do not need to be run destructively to prove it** — reproducing a
known mechanism by causing more unintended writes would be evidence-gathering theatre.

The requirement is **not** "add `init --help`." That is one symptom, and fixing only the symptom
leaves every other misspelling live. The invariant is closer to: *unknown or invalid arguments must
fail closed before any mutation*. Design and implementation are deliberately left to v1.1 — the
question of whether unrecognised flags should be rejected on read-only commands too, and how that
interacts with the exit-code contract, has not been thought through.

### The half that must be preserved

The same invocation is also **positive evidence for a v1.0 property.** `project-policy.yml` and
`PROJECT.md` already existed and differed from the templates. Init refused both, reported them as
conflicts, and named the per-path opt-in. Nothing tracked was touched. The no-overwrite default is
asserted by a test; here it was exercised by a genuine mistaken invocation against a real repository
and it held. Whatever argument handling replaces the current parsing must not weaken it, and the
existence of a second guard is not a reason to relax the first — the two failed independently and
only one of them was designed for this.

## 0b. An INFERRED heuristic must not reach an unclearable verdict

**Status: evidence-backed, confirmed by two producers, and the most consequential entry here — with
[§0i](#0i-ledger-grammar-is-positional-and-converts-careful-prose-into-invariant-violations).**
Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-post-init.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-v1.0.0-post-init.md) §3,
raw output in `../artifacts/adoption/rh-postinit-validate.json`.

**The second producer's instance is worse in two ways.** In PvsNP a different rule —
`claims.silent-promotion`, a 48-character adjacency window over prose — blocks the entire run on this
sentence in the open-problem record:

> **The Cook–Levin theorem** (CLM-0009) — proved in the literature, formalised in Isabelle/HOL and
> Coq, not here.

The sentence denies the status in its own second clause. The mechanism cannot distinguish *the name
of a famous theorem* from *an assertion of status*, so **a project whose subject matter is named
theorems will hit it constantly** — this is structural, not incidental.

And the finding is labelled **`OBSERVED`**, while `computation.error-bounds-stated` in the same run
is correctly `INFERRED`. A heuristic asserting certainty about its own output is the failure
`evidence.labels` exists to name, occurring inside the tool. That strengthens the requirement: it is
not only that an `INFERRED` finding must not reach an unclearable verdict, but that **a heuristic must
not be able to label itself `OBSERVED` in the first place.**

### The observation

Immediately after initialization, with an untouched template policy, the specimen validated as
`BLOCKED_BY_INVARIANT` — the framework's terminal conclusion, the one Standard 22 requires an agent
to stop on. The blocking finding:

```text
rule      computation.evidence-as-proof
severity  warning
label     INFERRED
message   5 passage(s) describe something as proved numerically or computationally.
```

All five matches are false positives. Two of them are the repository *specifying its own labelling
discipline*; three are descriptions of a theorem proved *about* a numerical scheme, which is the
correct direction of the very distinction the rule exists to protect.

### The mechanism

[`compliance.mjs:141`](../scripts/compliance.mjs) derives the result state from the **rule's level**:

```js
const outcome = level === "required" || level === "forbidden" ? RESULT.failed : RESULT.warning;
```

The finding's own `severity` and evidence `label` travel into the envelope for a human reader and are
then discarded by the verdict. Two correct v1.0 doctrines collide:

```text
heuristics are warning + INFERRED        so they can never masquerade as observations
forbidden + nonExemptible failures       block absolutely, and nothing may clear them
```

Each is right on its own. Together they make the unclearable verdict reachable by a regex over prose.

### Why unclearability makes it serious rather than merely annoying

An ordinary false positive is a nuisance the adopter routes around. This one has no route. The rule
has a subject, so not-applicable is false. An exception against a `nonExemptible` rule is rejected by
design. An attestation cannot clear an invariant — that is the whole point of the mechanism. The only
two actions available to a correct, scrupulous repository are to edit its honest prose, or to change
the framework. **The first is exactly what Standard 21 prohibits**: altering the work because the
standards system produced an unwanted conclusion. A verdict that can only be cleared by an act the
standards forbid is a defect in the standards, not in the adopter.

### The general property, worth keeping past this regex

> A verdict clearable only by an act the standards themselves prohibit is defective.

That is a test any future invariant-producing rule can be held to, and it is independent of what the
detector was looking for. It is also the sharper statement of what went wrong: epistemic strength
increased somewhere between evidence collection and conclusion. The detector said *I infer this prose
may exhibit X*; the engine concluded *X occurred, an invariant was violated, stop.* The uncertainty
was not weighed and discarded — it was never carried.

### Why the matches happened, characterized

Full analysis in
[`rh-postinit-characterization.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-postinit-characterization.md) Part 1.
The five are **two** semantic categories, not one and not five:

- **Role confusion** (4 of 5) — the evidence-word sits in an *argument* position of the proved
  statement rather than an *evidential* one. The rule targets `numerics ⟹ claim`; every instance is
  `proof ⟹ statement about numerics`. The direction is inverted.
- **Mention, not use** (1 of 5) — the words appear as an enumerated taxonomy (`proved / cited /
  numerical-only / asserted`), predicated of nothing.

The second is this repository's own use-versus-mention doctrine failing one level up: `structureOf()`
implements it lexically, over comments and string literals, and has nothing to say about prose that
discusses the vocabulary. The framework already grants itself exactly this exemption —
[`standards.mjs:989`](../scripts/standards.mjs) skips `standards/` because *these documents describe
the error* — and did not consider that an adopter's documents might need it too.

And the selection effect, which generalizes to every vocabulary-matching detector:

> A detector that pattern-matches on epistemic vocabulary has a false-positive rate that rises with
> the adopter's rigor. The more carefully a project distinguishes proof from numerics in writing, the
> more often it says both words in one sentence.

All five matches come from documents whose function is classifying the project's own evidence.

### What a fix has to get right

- The confidence label the detector already assigns must reach the verdict. An `INFERRED` finding is
  a prompt to look, not an observation, and the envelope already knows this.
- **Do not fix it by softening the invariant.** `BLOCKED_BY_INVARIANT` being unclearable is the
  property that makes a prohibition a prohibition, and weakening it to accommodate a bad detector
  would be the manipulation Standard 21 names, arriving through the front door.
- **Do not fix it by deleting the heuristic.** It has never been shown to be worthless in general —
  only that its output cannot carry this weight. Testing principle 3 cuts against removing a guard
  as readily as against adding one.
- The likely shape is that only `OBSERVED` findings may block, with `INFERRED` findings on invariant
  rules surfacing as something that demands human adjudication and resolves to an attestation — but
  that is a sketch, not a decision, and it needs the second adopter before it becomes one.

## 0c. A recognized representation of the required evidence must not be read as its absence

**Status: evidence-backed, confirmed by two producers.** Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/rh-slice-measurement.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-slice-measurement.md) §3.

**The second producer hit the same regex on the opposite side.** In PvsNP,
`computation.error-bounds-stated` fired on a claim ending *"…the counting bounds proved over
`Fin n → Option Bool` are about a different object."* The approximation pattern matched **`about`
used as an English preposition**; the suppressor missed `bounds` because it tests `\bbound\b`
singular. The claim is exact, finite, combinatorial and machine-checked — there is no approximation
and no bound to state.

So the rule now has two independent failures with the same cause and opposite polarity: it reads a
rigorous bound as an approximation lacking one (`O(`), and it reads an ordinary English word as a
declaration of approximation (`about`). The general shape both adopters named independently: **these
heuristics get noisier as the prose gets more rigorous**, because rigorous mathematical English uses
"about", "bound", "almost", "trivially" and "clearly" as ordinary words far more often than loose
prose does.

### The observation

`computation.error-bounds-stated` fires on two claims stating `c_n = O(n^{-3/4+ε})`.
[`standards.mjs:915`](../scripts/standards.mjs):

```js
const approximate = /≈|~=|\bapproximately\b|\babout\b|\bO\(|\bo\(|…/i.test(entry.statement);
const bounded     = /\berror\b|\bbound\b|\binterval\b|\bwithin\b|\bat most\b|\bprecision\b/i.test(…);
```

`O(` marks the statement approximate; the absence of the word "bound" marks it unbounded. But a
big-O statement is a rigorous universally-quantified inequality with an implied constant. **The `O`
*is* the error bound.** The detector recognizes the notation well enough to conclude an
approximation is present and not well enough to see that the same token supplies what it then
demands.

### The requirement, stated generally

The fix is **not** "recognize big-O" — that is implementation-shaped and would leave every sibling
case live. The evidence supports:

> A detector must not treat a recognized mathematical representation of the required evidence as
> evidence of that requirement's absence.

Sibling cases the same statement covers: an interval-arithmetic certificate satisfying a
precision requirement, a `Θ`/`Ω` bound, a formal statement whose hypotheses *are* the validity
conditions a rule asks to see stated.

This is the third rigour inversion in the record, and the first that is not a prose heuristic — see
§0b for the prose arm and §0 for the reference-boundary case. A project writing
`c_n = O(n^{-3/4+ε})` is being more precise than one writing "decays fast", and only the first is
flagged.

## 0d. Evidence types must mean the same thing to every rule

**Status: evidence-backed.** Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/rh-slice-measurement.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-slice-measurement.md) §3.

### The observation

`proof.counterexample-search-recorded` fires on a `THEOREM` whose evidence is `citation` plus
`literature-search` — Báez-Duarte's criterion, published in 2005 — demanding a counterexample search
of a result relied on as literature.

The cause is two constants that disagree:

```text
computation.evidence-as-proof   PROOF_EVIDENCE = {proof, formal, citation}
                                a citation is adequate support at proved rank
proof.counterexample-search…    skip list     = {counterexample-search, proof, formal, computational}
                                a citation is not evidence of anything
```

### The requirement

Not "add `citation` to one skip set." The declared evidence vocabulary is a shared type system, and
two rules currently assign the same declared type different epistemic weight by accident of where
the constant was written.

> Evidence types carry one meaning across the catalog. A rule that needs a different reading of a
> type must declare the deviation explicitly, with its reason, rather than encode it in a private
> constant.

A rule genuinely may need to deviate — `formal` evidence means something different to a placeholder
check than to a status check — so the requirement is explicitness, not uniformity.

## 0e. Attestations do not go stale unless the operator guesses a digest

**Status: evidence-backed.** Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/rh-attestation-measurement.md`](../artifacts/adoption/rh-attestation-measurement.md) §5.

The schema's own guidance:

> "Omit it on a first pass — the validator reports the current digest so it can be recorded."

**It does not.** Fourteen attestations were written against real paths with no `digest`; neither the
human render, the `status` summary, nor the JSON result contains the current digest for any of them.
[`compliance.mjs`](../scripts/compliance.mjs) only checks staleness `if (against?.digest)`, so an
attestation without one is permanently fresh and nothing says so.

Staleness is the property that keeps an attestation honest as the work moves — the pre-adoption
baseline named it as the main thing v1.0 has that RH's review cycle lacks. It is opt-in, the
documented way to opt in is broken, and the resulting silence looks identical to an attestation that
is genuinely current. **The verdict should be able to distinguish *reviewed and still current* from
*reviewed, currency unknown*.**

## 0h. The framework audits itself inside the adopter's compliance score

**Status: evidence-backed, second producer.** Evidence: PvsNP adoption findings F-05 and F-06.

`integrity.provenance-digest` reads `artifacts/provenance-digests.json` from **the framework's own
home directory**, not the adopter's. It passes on every adopter run, and what it certifies is that
MathematicsStandards' source documents are unmodified — a true statement about the framework,
reported inside another project's score. `integrity.rule-lifecycle-honest`,
`agent.explainable-findings` and `evidence.skipped-never-passed` are the same shape.

This is a **third category** the §0 entry does not separate. A `passed` row is currently one of:

```text
1. evaluated, evidence present, clean      a real check on real evidence
2. evaluated, nothing to examine           the §0 defect
3. not about this project at all           this entry
```

All three print identically and all three enter the denominator. The requirement is not "exclude
these rules" — the framework auditing its own integrity during an adopter's run is *useful*. It is
that **a result must say whose property it is about.**

## 0i. Ledger grammar is positional, and converts careful prose into invariant violations

**Status: evidence-backed, second producer. Ranks with §0b as most urgent.** Evidence: PvsNP adoption
findings F-07, both instances hit during authoring.

```text
- **Obligations:** none
    → no obligations. Correct.

- **Obligations:** none — the claim is an implication and the implication is complete
    → ONE OBLIGATION, named "none — the claim is…", in the OPEN state.
      isNone requires the trimmed value to be exactly `none`;
      parseObligation defaults unrecognised text to `open`.
    → on a proved-rank claim: proof.complete-with-open-obligations, an invariant.
```

`parseHistory` shares the shape: everything after the **first comma** inside the trailing parentheses
is taken as the evidence, so a history line whose reason contains no comma records *no evidence* and
trips `claims.silent-promotion` — again an invariant.

Neither is a false positive. In both cases the tool reported something true about its own parse. The
defect is what the author must do about it: **explaining why a field is empty converts the field into
a violation, and the remedy is to write less.** That is §0b's general property — *a verdict clearable
only by an act the standards themselves discourage* — reached through the parser rather than a regex,
which is why the two rank together.

Two requirements, both stated away from implementation:

1. **A parse that changes a field's meaning must be visible in the output.** "One open obligation
   named `none — the claim is…`" is a diagnosable message; "1 open obligation" is not.
2. **A grammar that produces invariant violations must be specified, not demonstrated.** Both rules
   here exist only as examples in a template. Neither the standard nor the schema states them.

## 0j. There is no state for "a machine established this part; the judgement is outstanding"

**Status: evidence-backed, second producer.** Evidence: PvsNP adoption finding F-09.

Two checks a machine can settle completely — every declaration named in a `Formal` block exists as a
`theorem` at its cited path; neither computational artifact uses floating point — bear directly on
`formal.helper-as-target` and `computation.float-as-exact`. The second has a detector and was
credited. The first has exactly one place to go: an `attestation`, whose semantics are *a human
established this*.

The consequence is a standing **pressure to write attestations that overstate their basis.** That
adopter declined and recorded the checks in prose instead, where no tool reads them.

v1.0 has one slot for non-automated evidence, and at least three things need it: human expert review,
an agent's mechanical check, and an agent's judgement. This is [§0l](#0l-an-attestation-does-not-say-what-kind-of-reviewer-made-it)
seen from the other side.

## 0k. Applicability is two-valued and a third value recurs

**Status: evidence-backed, second producer.** Evidence: PvsNP's classification of all 29 manual rules
— applicable 24, not-applicable 1, **unclear 4**.

`unclear` is not hedging, and the four instances share one structure: **the rule's failure mode is
impossible inside the formal development and entirely possible in the prose around it.** An induction
in Lean cannot omit its base case; an induction sketched in a design document can.
`proof.edge-cases-addressed`, `proof.unjustified-triviality`, `proof.induction-gaps` and
`rigor.symbolic-validity-conditions` all split that way.

Declaring them applicable overstates the risk across ~63,000 lines where the failure cannot occur;
declaring them not-applicable is false. The field admits neither answer, so the classification lives
in a document no tool reads.

Note this is **not** an argument for a general "partially applicable" value. The observed shape is
specific: a rule whose subject is split across a machine-checked artifact and its surrounding prose.

## 0l. An attestation does not say what kind of reviewer made it

**Status: evidence-backed, confirmed by two producers — who resolved it in opposite directions.**
Evidence: [`../artifacts/adoption/two-adopters.md`](../artifacts/adoption/two-adopters.md) §1 H6.

Standard 19 R6 records who, when, what was examined, and what it was reviewed against. It records
nothing about *what kind of reviewer* or *how independent*.

Given the same gap, two careful adopters did opposite things:

- One wrote 14 attestations — including two rejections, one of which found a real overclaim nothing
  else in that project would have caught — under a policy header stating every one was authored by an
  AI agent and is not independent human expert review. **The disclosure lives in a YAML comment no
  tool reads.**
- One wrote **zero**, on the ground that an agent's file scan is not a human's warrant, quoting
  `INSTRUCTIONS.md`: *"Do not attest to something you did not check."* **The abstention is
  indistinguishable in the output from a project that never looked.**

Both are defensible under v1.0, which is the finding: **v1.0 underdetermines what an attestation
means.** The first adopter's assurance is real and unlabelled; the second's caution is real and
invisible.

What is confirmed is the need for **reviewer kind**. What remains an observation, not a requirement,
is *independence*: one specimen closes contracts on four independent adversarial reviews and has good
reason to model it; that does not establish that every mathematical review needs an independence
model, and no second producer has asked for one.

## 0f. Observed representational gap — scope can be a point

**Status: observed, not yet a defect.** `computation.scope-declared` requires computational evidence
to state a range and an arithmetic. CLM-0010 is a certified interval-arithmetic bound at a **single
parameter value**, deliberately; its `Quantifiers` field says so and the detector does not read it.

A scope may legitimately be a point rather than an interval. Whether the model needs generalizing —
a `scope: point` shape, or reading `Quantifiers` — is not decided by one claim. Recorded so the
second adopter, or the remaining ~50 RH claims, can say whether it recurs.

## 0g. Observed: the policy format cannot hold an attestation's reasoning

**Status: evidence-backed, confirmed by two producers.** In PvsNP it landed harder: a `project-policy.yml`
using `>-` for a multi-line `reason:` was rejected outright — `line 52: block scalars are not
supported`, `NOT_EVALUATED`, exit 2. The exit-code contract behaved exactly as designed, but `>-` is
the natural YAML form for a prose `reason:` or `revisitWhen:`, the schema's own field descriptions are
long prose, and neither `templates/project-policy.yml` nor `INSTRUCTIONS.md` warns against it. Every
value in that project's policy is now a single long quoted line, with a comment explaining why.

So the constraint bites in two places, not one: the attestation `evidence:` field *and* every
applicability `reason:`.

The vendored YAML reader rejects block scalars (`|`, `>`), and a quoted scalar
may not contain its own quote character. So the `evidence:` field — where an attestation's entire
substance lives — must be **one physical line with no double quotes**. Fourteen attestations averaging
roughly 700 characters were folded onto single lines, and every quotation from the reviewed documents
had its quote marks stripped.

The strictness is deliberate and right: the parser's own header argues that a reader which quietly
ignores a construct is worse than one that refuses it. But the reference template only ever showed
one-sentence attestations, so the constraint never surfaced. A mechanism intended to carry expert
reasoning has a format that punishes reasoning at length.

## 1. Ledger reconciliation against a formal development

### The problem

The claims ledger assumes claims are registered by hand, and at fixture scale that is obviously
right. In a substantial Lean development there are hundreds or thousands of declarations: helper
lemmas, `simp` lemmas, instances, generated declarations, implementation details, and library-facing
results that are not claims the *project* is making about mathematics.

The failure mode is drift in one direction. The ledger stays a curated record of what the project
claims — which is the entire source of its signal — or it degrades into a stale partial mirror of
what the development contains, which is worse than no ledger because it looks complete.

### What must not be built

**A generator that imports every declaration.** It would destroy the signal it was meant to protect:
a ledger where a `MACHINE_CHECKED_PROOF` of the target sits among four hundred `simp` lemmas conveys
nothing. It would also answer, on the project's behalf, the one question the ledger exists to ask —
what status does the evidence support? — which is the same reason `init` refuses to generate a ledger
for a project that already has mathematics.

### What is worth designing instead

A reconciliation check that triages rather than counts:

```text
Lean source declarations
        │
        │ reconciliation
        ▼
Claims Ledger
        │
        ├── accounted for
        ├── intentionally untracked
        └── unexplained declaration
```

The third branch is a **prompt**, not a violation. Getting that wrong reinvents exactly the
false-positive problem the 29 manual-review rules were left undetected to avoid.

### The open question that has to be answered first

**Which formal declarations are significant enough to require ledger representation?**

Until that has an answer grounded in a real repository, the check cannot be built without guessing.
Candidate signals, none yet validated:

- Declarations referenced by the project's research prose.
- Exported or public target results, as against internal lemmas.
- Declarations reachable in the dependency chain of an already-registered formal claim.
- Declarations the project explicitly designates as targets, in the policy or by attribute.

The last is the most honest and the least automatic, which given this repository's other decisions is
probably the right starting point rather than the fallback. An `intentionally untracked` declaration
also needs somewhere to be recorded, and that record needs the same treatment as a not-applicable
classification: a reason, and something that would change it.

### Answered by two adopters — and the answer argues against building the check

**Both adopters answered the significance question the same way, independently, and neither answer is
mechanical.** Evidence:
[`../artifacts/adoption/two-adopters.md`](../artifacts/adoption/two-adopters.md) §1 H1.

> A claim is a place where a reader could reasonably ask "how do you know?" and the answer is not
> "read the next line of Lean". A declaration is normally **evidence for** a claim, not a claim.

Of the four candidate signals above, the two adopters between them **falsified the first two**. RH
measured that 781 of its 987 declarations are named somewhere in research prose, but 799 of 970 appear
only in log documents, and its three most important synthesis documents name **zero** declarations —
so "referenced by prose" selects almost everything and its negation selects the wrong things.
Exported-vs-internal fails for the same reason in a library organised by namespace.

The measured ratios:

```text
RH     ~50-60 of   987 declarations   ~5%
PvsNP      24 of 3,489 declarations   0.69%
```

The unit is **absolute, not proportional.** A proportional rule would have given PvsNP ~175 claims.
The number of places a project's epistemic weight rests is a property of the research programme, not
of how much has been formalised — so a coverage-shaped check would be wrong at exactly the scale where
it is most tempting.

Both adopters also named the pressure this check would create. PvsNP wrote it into its ledger preamble
*and* its `AGENTS.md`: **"the pressure to inventory is structural — the tool counts claims, and a
project that wants a bigger number can always produce one."**

That does not kill the entry, but it reshapes it. The fourth candidate signal — declarations the
project explicitly designates — is now the only one with support, and the remaining design question is
narrower than the one posed above: **not which declarations must be registered, but how a project
records that a declaration was considered and deliberately not registered**, with a reason and
something that would change it. The other three signals should not be built.

### Why the fixtures cannot settle this

Every fixture here is small enough that hand-registration is the obvious approach, so none of them
exhibits the pressure that makes reconciliation necessary. This is a gap that only adoption reveals,
which is why it is a candidate and not a task.

## 2. What real adoption should pressure-test

In rough order of how much the design is exposed:

| Component | The question a real repository answers |
| --- | --- |
| Claims ledger | Does hand-registration survive contact with hundreds of declarations, and where does it stop being worth it? |
| Dependency graph | Do researchers actually declare `Depends`, or does the graph quietly become decorative? |
| Proof obligations | Is the enumerate-and-discharge model usable during a proof, or only after one? |
| Formal chain detection | How often is `formal.placeholder-in-chain` right, and how often is the placeholder somewhere the claim does not depend on? |
| Open-problem lifecycle | Do the nine required sections get answered, or filled in? |
| Attestations | Do they get written at all? They carry most of the framework's real assurance and cost the most effort |
| `BLOCKED_BY_INVARIANT` | When a real project is blocked, is fixing-or-stopping actually what happens? |

### Attestation adoption — the first metric worth collecting

The attestation row is the one to watch, and unlike the others it has a measurement. Twenty-nine
rules are satisfiable only by human review, so v1.0's practical assurance depends not on having the
mechanism but on researchers using it.

**Of the applicable manual-review rules: how many were actually reviewed, how many remain
`not-evaluated`, how long did a review take, and how quickly did attestations go stale as the
research changed?**

Four readings, each pointing somewhere different — which is what makes it worth measuring rather than
just noting:

| Observation | Likely diagnosis | Where the work goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low attestation, rules felt irrelevant | Applicability is under-used or too coarse | Applicability, not the attestation mechanism |
| Low attestation, rules felt relevant | The workflow is too burdensome | Tooling and ergonomics |
| High attestation, frequent staleness | `reviewedAgainst.paths` scope is drawn too broadly | Attestation scope, not the digest rule |
| High completion, findings people acted on | The mechanism works | Leave it alone |

**What must not happen to this metric.** "29 manual rules, 2 actually reviewed" has to stay visibly
different from "29 manual rules passed". The skipped-never-passed invariant already guarantees that
in the verdict, and the temptation on seeing a low number will be to close the gap by making rules
auto-pass, by lowering their level, or by giving them weak detectors. The number is the finding. The
right response is to measure the gap accurately, not to make it smaller.

## 3. Explicitly not planned

- **More detectors to raise `frameworkCoverage`.** See testing principle 3.
- **More standards.** The 22 cover the source and the charter. A 23rd would need a failure it
  addresses that none of the existing 22 does.
- **Network-backed citation checking.** It would make `literature.fabricated-citation` detectable and
  make every audit non-deterministic and dependent on a third party's uptime. The rule states its
  limit instead.
- **A profile mechanism** ("strict mode", "publication mode"). Applicability already allows per-rule
  tailoring, and a named profile hides which rules it turned off.
