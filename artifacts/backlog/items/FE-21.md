## What

Stop rules reading a present, correct representation of required evidence as its absence.

## Why

Replicated. RH: an `O(` marks a statement approximate when the O is the bound. PvsNP: `\babout\b` matched an English preposition, and the suppressor missed `bounds` against `\bbound\b`.

## Notes

Deliberately excluded from Tier 1: the engine should stop treating weakly supported findings as certainty before a detector is asked to carry the epistemic safety of the whole system. Adjacent to §0d — a shared evidence-recognition model would address both.

**That last prediction was tested and is half wrong.** §0c reads free prose and §0d reads a closed nine-token vocabulary; a recogniser over English and a capability table are not one mechanism, and forcing them together would produce something that reads regexes out of a table. What they do share is a discipline — in both, what counts as the evidence was written down twice and the copies drifted — so they got two declarations and one shared contract test, which is the piece that would have caught both.

Implemented on branch tier3-semantic-consistency, unmerged. Markers are declared once with what they assert and what they carry, read in one pass over one text; the v1.2.0 pair read two different strings, which is how a marker could assert over the statement and be unable to discharge over the statement plus its evidence. Both recorded arms are gone from both specimens: RH's `c_n = O(n^{-3/4+ε})` on CLM-0004 and CLM-0005, and PvsNP's CLM-0021, whose one sentence contains both PvsNP arms — the plural `bounds` the suppressor could not match and the preposition `about` the trigger should never have matched.
