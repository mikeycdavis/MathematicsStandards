## What

Make an attestation's staleness visible: the schema says the validator prints the digest and it does not.

## Why

Single adopter, and serious out of proportion to its size. Staleness is what makes a historical human judgement usable as current assurance.

## Notes

An assurance that silently stops being true is a defect of the same kind as Tier 1, one step further from the verdict.

**The recorded mechanism was wrong, and the branch corrected it against the frozen RH specimen.** This item said the digest is "not printed in the render, the summary, or the JSON". Measured, the human render prints all 14; `status` and `--json` print none. The adopter measured from `--json`, so they were right about their observation and wrong about the mechanism — and the consequence, 14 permanently-fresh attestations, is exactly as real either way. The defect is a value that exists and is correct being emitted on one output surface of three, and not the machine-readable one.

Implemented on branch tier2-evidence-provenance. An attestation carries `currency: verified-current | unknown` with `currentDigest`, and the human render, `status` and `--json` each state it in their own idiom. The acceptance property is not that the framework knows the currency but that no supported surface lets an unmeasured attestation read as a current one; the phrase and the count are derived in one place each so the three cannot drift apart again.

This sat at IN_PROGRESS for one round because the first implementation stopped at the model and `status` said nothing — the same defect in miniature, on the surface an agent is most likely to read. Closed to IN_REVIEW in c03aa28, with a single falsifier over all three surfaces rather than three tests, mutation-checked in both directions. Both frozen specimens unmoved: RH `BLOCKED_BY_INVARIANT` / 88, PvsNP `NON_COMPLIANT` / 97, and RH's `status` now reports 12 of 12 attestations of unknown currency where it reported none.
